# V7 — Transfer-Aware Food Mass, Energy and Intake Estimation

**Status:** Corrected engineering and preregistration-ready *draft*. No accuracy, clinical, or release claim is made.  
**Version date:** 2026-11-07  
**Supersedes:** V6 — Transfer-Aware Food Mass and Calorie Estimation  
**Intended product:** Optional-assistance, non-clinical food logging on Android; **not** a medical nutrition-measurement device. A different intended use requires a new risk analysis and acceptance contract.  
**Primary objective:** Minimize error in **estimated energy actually consumed** under a realistic user workflow, while separately measuring served energy, visual food recognition, portion mass, nutrient-code selection, recipe uncertainty, workflow success and user effort.

> **Evidence boundary:** Public datasets establish model performance only under their acquisition, labeling and split protocols. Measured ingredients plus nutrient databases yield *recipe-derived reference energy*, not chemically measured or physiologically metabolized energy. All numerical targets identified as **proposed** below are planning assumptions, not observed system performance.

## 1. Decisions and falsifiable hypotheses

1. **Default to one photograph.** A second view is an experimental option, not a requirement; adopt it only if it improves a frozen, full-workflow calorie metric enough to justify additional friction.
2. **Make complete-meal and consumed-energy error primary.** Do not use matched-food mass MAE as a substitute. Score missed, extra, misidentified and badly matched food components.
3. **Treat vision-only identifiability as limited.** Hidden fat, recipe composition, cooking methods and selective leftovers can produce different energy totals from visually equivalent images. No image geometry model can resolve information that is absent.
4. **Start with the simplest competitive architecture:** VLM-assisted food recognition, explicit ingredient/preparation choices, direct mass/serving priors, nutrient database lookup and manual correction. A VLM-conditioned direct mass regressor is the first trained candidate. Latent height, patch ownership, metric scale, predicted depth and multiview geometry must each show incremental value in controlled comparisons.
5. **Ask at most one high-value preparation question by default,** but allow user-initiated additional corrections. Compare question, second view, reference object and manual portion input by expected improvement *per unit of effort*.
6. **Offer an opt-in repeat-meal route.** Store a user-confirmed reference and editable portion multiplier; verify meal identity and recipe changes. A prior estimate alone must not become self-reinforcing calibration ground truth.
7. **Use deployment-like weighed meals as final product evidence.** Separate public-dataset exploration, phone measurement, pilot design and frozen external validation.
8. **Do not publish numeric confidence intervals before validation.** Even valid split conformal intervals require an appropriate exchangeability assumption; shift and conditional coverage problems require separate monitoring and explicit limitations.
9. **No data/model use without documented rights and privacy review.** Dataset, code, model weights, hosted API terms and individual photo/consent permissions have distinct obligations.
10. **Freeze product acceptance criteria before locked validation.** An inconclusive result is not a pass, and favorable benchmark results cannot override failure of a final use-case gate.

### Research hypotheses (not conclusions)

- **H1:** One-view, VLM-conditioned direct mass regression improves complete-meal calorie MAE compared with a simple food-prior baseline on unseen users, foods and devices.
- **H2:** Matched-input latent geometry, physical scale, explicit depth or a second image provide incremental gains beyond the direct baseline on specific, preregistered subgroups.
- **H3:** A targeted question about ingredients/preparation reduces absolute calorie error more per unit of user effort than an extra image for visually ambiguous dishes.
- **H4:** User-confirmed weighed repeat meals improve prediction on genuinely later meals without increasing error when recipes, bowls or portions change.
- **H5:** Models that work on Nutrition5k degrade under device/food/framing shift; appropriately sampled local data and user correction may reduce this degradation.

## 2. Product flow and the limits of observation

```text
Take one photo
    |
    v
Recognize visible foods and estimate served amounts
    |
    +--> Photo inadequate / unknown food --> Retake or manual entry
    |
    +--> Likely repeat meal --> Confirm recipe + editable serving multiplier
    |
    +--> High-value ambiguity --> ONE targeted preparation/quantity question
    |
    +--> Optional second view --> Only in study/validated supported settings
    v
Show components, selected nutrient records, assumptions,
served kcal/macros and clearly indicated uncertainty
    |
    v
At eating completion: All / 3/4 / Half / Other
    |                     |
    |                     +--> Optional component-specific leftovers / photo
    v
Show *estimated* consumed kcal/macros and editable history
```

**Do not confuse served with consumed.** Before leftovers are known, the system can estimate what was *served*. It cannot know what the user will consume. `served_energy × selected_fraction` is an explicitly approximate interface shortcut, not a measured intake observation. For nonuniform leftovers, permit component-level selection or manual grams where available.

**Visually unidentifiable information:** A photo may not distinguish 400 kcal from an otherwise identical serving containing 20 g hidden oil (~180 kcal extra). With equal probability of these two visually indistinguishable cases, any image-only point estimator has expected absolute error at least 90 kcal: `(|x-400|+|x-580|)/2 >= 90`. This is an illustrative information-theoretic counterexample, not an estimate of real-world prevalence. Such cases must be separately labeled in the evaluation.

**UI principles:** Make assumptions editable; show what is known vs guessed; minimize retakes, data collection and prompts; present estimates as approximate, not clinical facts; do not silently infer particular health conditions or require geolocation. Preserve a manual logging fallback even when the model abstains.

## 3. Evaluation units, reference standards and data collection

### 3.1 Units and leakage protection

- **Person/household ID:** privacy-preserving pseudonym. A household can repeat recipes and share vessels.
- **Meal instance ID:** a specific physical meal served on a date. Every crop, camera frame, two-view pair, leftover photo and alternative prompt belongs to that instance.
- **Recipe ID / physical vessel ID / capture session ID / device ID:** enable shift audits and grouped splitting.
- **Component ID:** edible ingredient or food component, with a consistent mapping between reference recipe and prediction where possible.
- **No frame-level random splitting.** Treat plate sequences, augmented frames and repeated capture sessions from one meal as a group; audit near duplicates and user/recipe overlap.

Retain both: (a) a **known-user, new-meal** test (realistic repeat-use) and (b) an **unseen-person/household** test (cold-start generalization). Add prespecified unseen-device, unseen-recipe and unseen-cuisine slices. Avoid implying these are statistically independent unless the sampling scheme actually ensures it.

### 3.2 Reference-label protocol for phone meals

1. Before serving, record weighed edible ingredients and their nutrient data source, code, version, unit and preparation state. Record additions (oils, sauces, sweeteners), discarded cooking liquids, cooking yield and relevant packaging data. Where practical, independently weigh the **served cooked components**, plate/vessel tare and added garnishes.
2. After eating, weigh edible leftovers and, if needed, discarded edible portions **by component**. Record spillage, sharing, second helpings and uncertain attribution. Use `served - leftover` *only* when quantities and identities permit this subtraction.
3. Construct **recipe-derived served and consumed kcal** from documented ingredient energy and defensible ingredient/component allocation. Track reference uncertainty (e.g. missing absorbed oil, variable nutrient values, inseparable mixed leftovers). Treat labels with unresolved uncertainty as lower-confidence or unscorable for precise consumed-energy validation.
4. Collect the app's *actual* user answers and photos without showing the annotator the model output; keep weighed reference labels hidden from the app and test engineer until the model and decision thresholds are frozen.
5. Record image metadata, camera model/physical lens, light, distance proxy, crop, focus/zoom, vessel, recipe change flags, participant interaction time, prompts, retakes, failures and manual corrections.
6. Check reference-label consistency with a second person or independent reconciliation on a prespecified subset. Retain an audit log rather than replacing inconvenient examples.

**Reference quality tags:** `A` = documented ingredients and well-weighed served and component leftovers; `B` = total mass reliable but mixture/leftover allocation uncertain; `C` = incomplete recipe or leftover labels. Report A-only sensitivity results and A+B results. Define exclusions **before** seeing model errors, and always report excluded fractions.

| Level | Data | Purpose | Cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|

### 3.4 Public datasets and permitted use

| Source | Scientific role | Evidentiary limit | Rights state / action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nutrition5k | Controlled measured-food mass and multi-camera geometry ablations | Fixed California cafeteria/rig; incremental plates; public split is a starting point, not phone validity | Repo links CC BY 4.0; record exact files, attribution and intended use |
| NutritionVerse-Real | Real-life multi-angle food scenes and ingredient weights | 889 photos represent only 251 distinct dishes; not multi-Android evidence | Verify specific dataset-host terms and commercial training rights **before download for that purpose** |

| FPB | Out-of-domain weighed item recognition/mass stress test | Multi-item trays, regional menu, different labeling and source distributions | Dataset card: CC BY-NC 4.0; no commercially directed use without appropriate permission/legal approval |
| MetaFood3D | Optional shape/mask unit tests | Mostly isolated objects, not real mixed-dish intake | CC BY-NC indicated by source; verify underlying assets and intended use |
| Own Android meals | Main release evidence | Sampling/recipe reference quality must be quantified | Participant consent, purpose limitation, security and deletion policy |

**Important paper-reading constraints:** Liao & Li (2026) report 16.7 g per-food MAE on Nutrition5k **for matched foods only**, with mass-weighted recognition recall 87% on Nutrition5k, 88% on NutritionVerse-Real and 64% on FPB. Their latent-height ablation is approximately 1.2 g (17.9 vs 16.7 g); VLM-derived context has a much larger effect (34.4 vs 16.7 g without that context). These results do not establish complete-meal calorie accuracy or cross-dataset deployment transfer. Lian (2026) shows severe cross-dataset gram-scale failure, but its external datasets differ in image preprocessing, food types and some label provenance: treat the findings as a deployment risk, **not** a causally isolated device-shift effect. Its 84% near-duplicate result concerns an internal random split; the reported official validation counterpart is 6%.

## 4. Controlled architecture and intervention experiments

### 4.1 Mandatory baselines

- **A0:** Food detection/recognition + food-specific serving prior + nutrient-record matching + user correction; no learned geometry.
- **A1:** Frozen VLM direct complete-meal/per-component portion estimate; one photo; fixed structured prompt and decoding. Evaluate with both predicted and oracle food identity where scientifically meaningful.
- **B0:** **VLM-context-conditioned direct mass regressor** (per-component or complete-meal) with no explicit height or depth. This is the first trained candidate, not a predetermined winner.
- **B1:** Identical B0 inputs/training but with mass-supervised latent-height representation.
- **B2:** Identical B0 inputs/training but with structured patch ownership, then an additional B1+B2 combination.
- **C0:** B0 plus measured image scale/food footprint or vetted household-vessel information.
- **C1:** B0 plus monocular predicted depth, where properly licensed.
- **C2:** Separate second-view variants with same recognition/nutrient inputs and training data; test silhouettes, triangulated features and latent fusion separately.
- **R0:** User-confirmed repeat-meal route; compare against A0/B0 on later independent meals, with deliberate recipe/vessel changes.

**Not all comparisons are automatically causal.** For each increment, match (to the extent practical) recognition outputs, food and density priors, image encoder, annotations, data split, augmentation, training budget, tuning budget, target labels and decoding. Report parameter count and processing cost. A larger context-rich model beating a context-poor baseline is not proof that geometry helped.

### 4.2 Factorial tests that isolate why a model wins

1. **Food context × latent height:** 2×2 comparison of context on/off and height on/off; with otherwise matched model/training, plus ownership as a subsequent factor.
2. **Image count:** Same eligible meals and base architecture, one view vs one+side; capture both for every meal in the controlled technical trial even if the final user workflow is adaptive. Do not compare a selectively two-photographed easy subset against all one-view meals.
3. **Reference availability:** Unknown vessel vs measured vessel, marker absent/present, current user-provided weighed reference absent/present.
4. **Preparation information:** No question vs a predetermined recipe question vs manual ingredient confirmation, on the same meals; separately measure question burden.
5. **Target shift:** Hold other conditions fixed where possible while varying physical camera, crop/framing, lighting, vessel, food class, recipe and device processing. Also report the combined real-world shift without pretending its sources are isolated.

**Geometry caveat:** Do not call sensor depth an attainable smartphone upper bound; it is only a reference condition on compatible rigs. Visual hull is a potentially biased/occlusion-sensitive feature, not a guaranteed true volume or universal 27.3%-error construction.

### 4.3 Oracle error decomposition

Evaluate the full prediction pipeline **and** reruns with individual components corrected from ground truth:

- Oracle visible-food identities/segmentation, keeping remaining modules fixed.
- Oracle masses, keeping predicted food identities and nutrient matching fixed where a defined correspondence exists.
- Oracle nutrient codes/recipe components, keeping estimated masses fixed.
- Oracle consumed component fractions/leftovers, keeping served estimates fixed.

For component energy `E = m × d` (mass × kcal per g), the algebraic interaction is:

`(m_hat × d_hat) - (m × d) = (m_hat-m)×d + m×(d_hat-d) + (m_hat-m)×(d_hat-d)`.

Thus accurate total energy can hide offsetting mass and energy-density errors. Oracle runs locate bottlenecks but must not be presented as unique causal attribution where components interact or unmatched foods lack correspondence.

## 5. Metrics and mandatory reporting

### 5.1 Primary end-to-end endpoints

- **Primary user endpoint:** MAE of **recipe-derived consumed kcal** on independently labeled, scorable D1 meals, using the *complete intended interaction flow* (all detected and missed components, prompts and user-reported consumption). Every eligible meal remains in the denominator; also report failure/abstention separately. Define fallback scoring before validation.
- **Co-primary engineering endpoint:** MAE of **recipe-derived served kcal** before consumption input, on the same eligible D1 meals. This diagnoses photo/preparation inference independently of leftovers.
- **Always report:** signed bias, P90 absolute error, error by calorie size, subgroup MAE/P90, macro error, relative error only with a prespecified positive denominator floor, and 95% confidence intervals from an appropriate **clustered** design.

### 5.2 Recognition/portion and product metrics

- Complete-meal kcal error, **including missed/spurious foods**; item-level matched mass MAE **only as secondary**. Track unmatched energy/mass fraction, false component energy, energy-weighted recognition recall, coverage and macro consistency. Report match rules and ambiguous mappings.
- Food-code exact and hierarchical-match accuracy, preparation-state accuracy, cooking-fat double-count frequency, fraction-input error and repeat-meal mismatch rate.
- **Selective prediction:** automated answer rate, abstention rate, manual-correction rate and error-vs-coverage curves. Never present accuracy only on meals the model elects to answer.
- UX/device: first-capture success, retakes, p50/p95 tap-to-result, **full interaction completion** latency, user task completion, question burden, inference expense and network failure handling.
- Dataset rights compliance, unintended image retention and deletion test outcomes are **independent blocking gates**.

### 5.3 Unit of inference and uncertainty analysis

The unit is ordinarily a unique physical meal, with clustering by participant/household when participants supply multiple meals. Use a participant-cluster bootstrap or a suitable hierarchical method; compare models pairwise on identical eligible meals. Meals from the same plate sequence cannot be treated as independent observations. State exclusion policies and the number of independent participants, dishes and device types. Disaggregate failures rather than silently dropping them.

## 6. Pilot, power, preregistration and locked comparisons

1. **D0 pilot: 30 unique meals minimum for debugging**, not a claim of sufficient statistical power. Estimate the standard deviation of paired absolute-error differences and user/recipe clustering, plus annotation failure rates.
2. Before D1, **preregister** the primary population, reference-quality inclusion policy, primary endpoint, all model/threshold versions, intervention policy, device strata, minimum benefit, sample size, cluster strategy, bootstrap method and multiplicity control for many candidates.
3. Illustrative unclustered normal approximation: for a 15 kcal paired mean-error improvement, SD of paired differences 100 kcal, two-sided α=0.05 and 80% power, `n ≈ ((1.96+0.84)×100/15)^2 ≈ 349` independent paired meals. This is **not** a study-size recommendation without pilot variance, design effect and attrition estimates.
4. Choose the best candidate on development/validation data, then freeze it and test on a fresh, untouched held-out D1 cohort. Do not iteratively tune on the locked final set or reuse it as calibration data.
5. **Second-photo technical gate:** compared against the frozen best one-photo workflow, require (a) preregistered practically meaningful absolute kcal gain, (b) at least a **provisional** 10% relative consumed-kcal MAE improvement, (c) a paired, cluster-aware confidence interval excluding zero on the favorable side, and (d) no unacceptable tail/failure deterioration. The final numerical criteria must be approved before D1.
6. **Second-photo UX gate:** separately randomize/compare the end-user workflows (optional second view vs one view), analyzing all assigned meals, including refusal, timeout, retakes and fallback. An offline two-view benefit alone does **not** justify a mandatory app step.
7. Test the **recipe question** by the same paired or randomized principles; compare improvement per interaction second and in absolute kcal. Do not assume it always wins.
8. Record experiment seeds, retries, hosted model/prompt version and raw structured response; budget API and license changes into reproducibility reports.

## 7. Android capture and physical geometry

1. Implement CameraX one-photo capture concurrently with dataset benchmarking; avoid a long-running full-resolution video ring buffer in MVP.
2. At minimum test **two materially different Android devices** (different physical camera configuration and processing pipeline). Document camera-specific performance instead of inferring general smartphone validity.
3. Log physical camera/lens ID when accessible, crop and zoom, resolution, orientation, focal length, focus/exposure state, intrinsics/distortion metadata if provided, timestamp and image sharpness. Do not assume Android exposes complete current intrinsics on every device.
4. Estimate and validate calibrated intrinsics with multiple checkerboard/ChArUco positions/distances when needed. Model focus-conditioned changes or mark physical scale low confidence; a single ArUco marker is not a universal intrinsic calibration.
5. Use autofocus with measured settling and sharpness, unless experiments show a fixed focus works at both viewpoints. Prevent lens-switch or crop mismatch from silently corrupting geometry.
6. For second views, independently estimate camera poses and test reprojection, baseline and per-region triangulation angle; reject or abstain on unreliable geometry. No hard-coded geometric threshold without hardware validation.
7. Assess scale reference as optional (reference object, measured vessel and known plate profiles). Record missing-reference rate and net effect on user completion.
8. Separate processing latency from capture, question and user correction latency; report **full workflow** results on low and higher capability devices, and degraded/offline behavior.

## 8. Food recognition, nutrient mapping and intake calculations

- Use a pinned, rights-reviewed detection/segmentation model and a structured food-recognition step. SAM 2 is only a mask candidate; it is not a recipe or identity oracle. Review checkpoint/weight licenses separately from code licenses. Avoid incompatible use of AGPL software or weights without legal clearance.
- Retain top candidate food codes, source database/version, preparation state, portion-unit conversions and confidence/abstention. Prefer exact recipe ingredients when known; do not silently substitute a generic food code for a branded or heavily prepared dish.
- Unit conventions must be explicit. For served energy use `E_served = Σ(m_i_grams × d_i_kcal_per_gram)` with components in the same cooked/raw preparation state as the nutrient record. Prevent oil/fat double counting; distinguish added fat from fat included in recipe or cooked-food nutrient records.
- For consumption use `E_consumed = Σ(m_i_consumed × d_i)`, where reference consumption is based on defensible measured leftovers. In the default UX `m_i_consumed` is inferred crudely from the selected fraction; disclose this limitation and provide component-level correction for selective eating.
- In matched-photo comparisons, freeze food identity, nutrient records, recipe question and consumption inputs when measuring *pure image geometry benefit*. Run a separate full-system comparison to measure the combined outcome.
- Implement explicit low-confidence routes for unknown food, poor focus, plate overflow, foods hidden by bowl walls, heavily mixed dishes, liquids and missed components. Human correction is an expected output, not a benchmark exclusion.

## 9. Uncertainty and monitoring

1. **MVP:** no numeric ±kcal claim. Show identified food, recipe assumptions, approximate kcal/macros and accessible correction. Qualitative uncertainty should explain what is unknown, without fabricated probability labels.
2. If supported by representative data, implement **precisely specified split conformal** intervals on an untouched calibration split with prediction target, nonconformity score, quantile rule, interval form and exchangeability assumptions recorded. Keep calibration data separate from model-selection and final-test samples.
3. Evaluate **marginal coverage and interval width** on an independent D1 locked test; report relevant subgroup empirical coverage and uncertainty, without claiming finite-sample distribution-free conditional coverage. Repeat for served and consumed targets separately where both are modeled.
4. The 2026 Lian paper's multiplicative-ratio intervals **resemble conformal intervals but lack its coverage guarantee**. Their nominal 90% coverage fell from 89.8% (in domain) to 32.1% (ASA24), 49.3% (ECUSTFD) and 0.9% (FPB). This is a caution about transfer, not a valid demonstration that exchangeable split-conformal theory failed.
5. No universal 250-calibration-meal minimum exists. For a simple independent binomial evaluation of 80% coverage to ±3 percentage points at approximate 95% confidence, `n≈1.96²×0.8×0.2/0.03²≈683` **test** examples. It does not establish the calibration-set size; clustered data need adjusted analysis.
6. Monitor device, camera/crop, cuisine, recipe, vessel, prediction/recognition confidence, correction and abstention patterns. Out-of-distribution detection can miss *visually indistinguishable hidden ingredients*; it is not a general accuracy detector.
7. If drift or validation failure occurs, **suspend numerical interval claims or invoke a separately validated conservative fallback**. Do not merely widen intervals arbitrarily and call them calibrated. Revalidate after model, code, nutrient-data or prompt updates.

## 10. Security, rights and system allocation

- **Cloud/x64 research:** legal/rights manifests, dataset processing, experiments, training, evaluation, audit and limited cloud inference. Ensure source data stay within their permitted uses.
- **Raspberry Pi agent:** optional ADB/Android test bridge, hardware-in-loop logging and on-device diagnostics. No need to store all raw datasets or perform GPU training on the Pi.
- **Android app:** capture, quality checks, opt-in upload, local-first logging, corrected portions, history and deletion/export UI.
- **Permissions manifest:** for each dataset, record source URL, version/checksum, attribution, precise data and derivative license, redistribution permission, commercial-training permission, evaluation-only restrictions, consent/privacy basis, expiry/review status and approval decision. Distinguish *research use* from legally permitted *noncommercial use*; CC BY-NC does not automatically authorize commercially directed R&D merely because it is labeled research.
- **Model manifest:** separate software, model-weight and hosted-service licenses/terms. Pin segmentation, VLM, depth, database and evaluation versions. Record API retention/training policy and prohibit silent use of user meal photos for provider training.
- **User data:** explicit cloud-processing disclosure, purpose limitation, encryption, access logging, deletion/export controls, no default GPS, no cross-user personalization leakage, and retention defaults subject to policy review. Evaluate Swiss FADP obligations and any relevant additional jurisdictions, particularly if dietary history reveals health information.
- **Reproducibility:** save commit, environment/dependency lock, dataset IDs, model IDs, prompts, raw responses where rights allow, random seeds, experiment config, costs and grouped prediction files. Version reference labels and corrections without erasing audit history.

## 11. Proposed release contract — must be approved BEFORE D1

The following values are **provisional internal planning targets**, not scientifically established tolerances or V7 performance assertions. Product stakeholders must approve or replace each with justified values tied to the explicitly **nonclinical self-logging** use case *before any locked testing*.

| Category | Proposed planning threshold | Gate logic |
|---|---|---|
| Full-workflow consumed-energy accuracy | MAE ≤ **150 kcal/meal** on reference-quality A D1 meals | Primary accuracy gate, must be reported alongside non-scorable fraction |
| Served-energy accuracy | MAE ≤ **120 kcal/meal** on A D1 meals | Separate engineering gate |
| Tail errors | P90 absolute consumed-energy error ≤ **350 kcal/meal** | Prevent average-only pass |
| Systematic error | Absolute mean signed consumed-energy bias ≤ **30 kcal/meal** | Report confidence interval and device/meal subgroup bias |
| Workflow task completion | ≥ **95%** of eligible attempts reach estimate or explicit manual fallback | Never exclude hard foods from denominator |
| Automatic answer rate | ≥ **80%** without compulsory manual entry | Coverage/utility trade-off; also show accuracy on those answers |
| User completion time | p95 ≤ **30 seconds**, including normal prompts/network under specified test conditions | Test lower-end and upper-end Android devices |
| Photo or model failure | ≤ **5%** require an unplanned retake or error recovery | Retakes still count toward completion/latency |
| Calibration / uncertainty | If intervals are shown: specified nominal marginal coverage and maximum practical width must pass independent tests | Otherwise show qualitative uncertainty only |
| Privacy/rights and intended use | **Zero unresolved blocking issues** | Hard gate independent of accuracy |

**Interpretation rules:** A passing point estimate alone is not enough; preregister uncertainty or one-sided confidence-bound rules where appropriate. Establish planned subgroup sample sizes and maximum unacceptable device/food-group deterioration. Define how abstentions/failures affect the primary metric: do not impute a favorable zero error. If an outcome cannot be evaluated, report it as not measured rather than counting it as a pass. If thresholds cannot be justified, label the release decision *undetermined* and conduct a decision study rather than changing thresholds after seeing D1 results.

**Final engineering stance:** Build a small, transparent one-photo system first; measure real reference intake and failure coverage; then add questions, memory, geometry or another image only when they cause reproducible end-to-end improvement on unseen users, meals and devices. Without independent consumed-energy validation, present the result as an approximate food-logging aid rather than a validated calorie-measurement system.
