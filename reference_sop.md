# Tier1 weighed-meal reference procedure

This is a **single-user exploratory** pilot of30 different physical meals on the owner's Android phone; record its actual model/OS with each session. The connected Samsung SM-G780G, Android13 is the internal runtime-test device. This pilot measures recipe-derived served energy, not chemical energy, medical accuracy, or generalization. Public-image experiments and synthetic unit fixtures do not count toward30. The Android collection is deferred and is not a prerequisite for the separate [public evaluation](docs/tier1-public-eval-2026-11-08/report.md).

1. Check a kitchen scale with a known check weight; record scale resolution/check, date, recipe, vessel tare and a pseudonymous meal/session/household ID. Include mixed dishes, sauces, drinks and difficult meals in the collection order. Do not select meals after seeing prediction errors. One photo per actual meal; retain original JPEG, SHA256 and available camera metadata.
2. Before cooking, weigh edible ingredient inputs. Record raw/cooked state and **actual** nutrient record or package label ID, version/snapshot, kcal/g and source. Record all oil, sauces and garnishes. Unknown nutrients are reference uncertainty, not database facts. Keep reference measurements hidden from inference.
3. Allocate ingredients to cooking batches with separate energy and mass fractions. An ingredient may enter several batches or plated components; total fractions cannot exceed1. Record edible cooking discards and justified energy losses separately. Evaporated/absorbed water changes mass and kcal/g, not energy. Do not invent fat retention measurements.
4. Weigh each cooked edible batch and record its homogeneity. For a reasonably homogeneous batch, retained energy = sum allocated input energy minus documented edible discards/losses; density = retained energy / weighed cooked batch mass. Allocate a served fraction using the independently weighed cooked serving. Example **arithmetic only**:100g dry ingredient at3.6kcal/g yields300g cooked;150g served contains180kcal, not540kcal.
5. For heterogeneous/selective servings, uniform kcal/g is not defensible. Weigh separable plated components or document independent ingredient allocations with evidence. A mixed homogeneous dish can be a single plated component made from many ingredients and still be Served-A. Never force ingredient IDs to match model food boxes one-to-one. Generic cooked database food must not overwrite a measured recipe.
6. Store the many-to-many mappings in `meal_schema.json`: ingredient allocations into batches, then source allocations into plated components. Each plated component's allocated cooked/source mass must reconcile with its weighed serving (scale tolerance: max0.5g or1%). Distinguish measured allocation from `homogeneous_yield`. Record allocation uncertainty; do not improve grades after seeing error.
7. Grade served and consumed references **independently**. Served-A has defensible recipe nutrients and weighed served allocation; B has material allocation uncertainty; C is incomplete. Consumed-A additionally needs explicit all-eaten confirmation with no sharing/spillage, or defensible edible leftovers. Unconfirmed intake is `unknown`, never0. Partial heterogeneous leftovers need independent leftover energy/component evidence. A mass fraction alone does not prove an equal calorie fraction. Record second helpings/sharing/spillage and lower the consumed grade when attribution is uncertain.
8. Capture and run A1 without revealing the reference weights. Keep the original prediction, returned model/version, prompt/schema/catalog versions, raw structured inventory, response tokens, inference latency, optional question/answer, failures, missing foods and later corrections. Record shutter-to-estimate, active interaction and wall-clock time separately; leave unmeasured fields null. Never replace original predictions with corrected results.
9. A0 in the supplied CLI uses **A1's food identities and preparation**, frozen generic serving priors, and the identical nutrient catalog/question answer. It is a portion-prior comparator, **not an independent recognition baseline**. Uncovered foods cause an explicit A0 coverage failure. Report pairing/attrition rather than excluding difficult meals. Freeze the catalog/model/prompt/priors for this pilot; record a new version if changed.
10. Copy CLI prediction rows into each meal's `predictions`. References must be prepared independently. Evaluate the whole meal, including missing/spurious foods, with `python3 evaluate_meals.py meals.json --output report.json`. Primary conditional accuracy uses all scorable Served-A successes, alongside coverage/failures on **all** meal attempts. Report A+B sensitivity, grade distribution, mixed meals, energy bins, identical-input pairs and manual-assisted outcomes separately. P90 uses linear interpolation; normalized absolute error divides by max(reference,200kcal). No confidence intervals or launch thresholds are inferred from30 meals.

At 1,5,10 and30 meals check reference feasibility and failure logs. The first practical experiment is one independently weighed mixed meal, then compare the original A0/A1 predictions before corrections. A model output or an old repeat estimate is never weighing ground truth.
